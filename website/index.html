Why It Matters

Your camera roll holds more than photographs.

It holds the evening your family stayed late around the table, the morning light from a trip you will never repeat, the ordinary Tuesday you didn’t realize would become a favorite memory five years later.

Phones get dropped, replaced, or run out of space. Yet backing up a decade of life usually leaves you with an uncomfortable choice: hand your library to a hosted photo company forever, or run and maintain a home server yourself.

Ambry — an old word for a recessed cabinet built into a wall to keep precious things — offers a quieter middle path.

How It Works

Four quiet steps from your camera roll to your private channels.

Ambry does not operate its own storage backend. Your phone handles the library work locally, while Telegram provides the underlying storage infrastructure.

Step 01

Connect a dedicated Telegram account

Sign in directly to Telegram inside Ambry using Telegram’s official TDLib client. A separate, dedicated Telegram account is recommended so your photo archive stays isolated from personal chats.

Step 02

Set your backup & encryption choices

Create your vault passphrase, record your 24-word recovery kit, and pick which Android albums to back up. Client-side encryption is on by default, and you can change it for new uploads anytime.

Step 03

Ambry processes your library on device

Your Android phone computes SHA-1 deduplication hashes, reads EXIF and GPS metadata, generates encrypted local thumbnails and previews, and updates your local SQLite library index.

Step 04

Media backs up to private channels

Ambry uploads each file to a private Library channel owned by your account and periodically saves encrypted index snapshots to a private System channel for recovery.

Privacy & Encryption

Your photos should stay yours.

Telegram private channels use client-server encryption, not end-to-end encryption. That is why Ambry encrypts your files on your Android phone before upload by default — while still letting you choose plain uploads if you prefer to open new items directly inside Telegram.

Enabled by default

Encrypted on your phone before leaving your device

Each photo or video gets its own unique AES-256-GCM key and is encrypted in 1 MiB segments before upload. Telegram stores opaque binary documents, so neither Telegram nor anyone opening your channel can view the images without your vault passphrase or 24-word recovery kit.

Channel sees: 8f3a1c90.bin · IMTG2:<sealed metadata>
Your explicit choice

Stored as ordinary files you can open in Telegram

If you switch off “Encrypt before upload” on the Backup screen, new uploads are stored as regular files inside your private channel. You can open them in Telegram directly — which also means anyone with access to your Telegram account, and Telegram itself, can view those unencrypted files.

Previously encrypted uploads & your library index/album names stay encrypted.

What default encryption protects

  • Photo and video contents, EXIF metadata, filenames, and captions are encrypted on device with AES-256-GCM before being sent to Telegram.
  • Your library index snapshots and album names are sealed with your vault key derived via Argon2id (passphrase) and HKDF (24-word recovery kit).
  • On-device thumbnails, the TDLib session database, and the unlocked vault key cache are encrypted using keys bound to the Android Keystore.
  • Zero Ambry analytics, zero advertising, zero crash reporting, and no developer servers ever receive your media or account details.

What encryption does not protect

  • Telegram still sees account-level metadata: your Telegram account, channel existence, upload timestamps, encrypted file sizes, and network connection.
  • Encryption does not protect against a compromised phone, unlocked device access, or an overly weak passphrase.
  • If you turn encryption off for new uploads, Telegram and anyone who accesses that Telegram account can view those unencrypted files.
  • Opening the Places map fetches map tiles from OpenFreeMap, which sees your IP address and the map region viewed (never your photos or personal data).

Recovery & Continuity

A new phone shouldn’t mean starting over.

Ambry backs up an encrypted snapshot of your library index to your private System channel daily (and on demand) so a new Android phone can reconstruct your timeline and albums from Telegram alone.

01 · Old phone

Daily index backup

Encrypted SQLite snapshots (newest 3 kept) and a pinned vault record live in your private System channel alongside media captions in your Library channel.

02 · Telegram storage

Vault discovery

Sign in to the same dedicated Telegram account on your new phone. Ambry locates the pinned System record automatically.

03 · New phone

Unlock with your key

Enter your vault passphrase or 24-word recovery kit. Ambry decrypts the newest index snapshot and reconciles any newer messages from channel history.

04 · Restored library

Timeline & albums return

Your timeline, albums, favorites, and metadata return. Thumbnails and originals are fetched from Telegram on demand as you browse.

What recovery requires — and honest limitations

  • Two things are required: access to the same Telegram account and either your vault passphrase or your 24-word recovery kit.
  • One active phone per vault: restoring on a new phone claims the vault so an old phone pauses writes and cannot accidentally overwrite newer albums or edits.
  • Lazy thumbnails after restore: in v1, restored thumbnails render on first scroll as originals are fetched from Telegram (packed thumbnail bundles are planned for a later update).
  • Pre-launch testing note: the restore pipeline is implemented and unit-tested; full end-to-end second-device restore testing is part of our closed testing checklist prior to public Google Play release.
No recovery backdoor: If you lose both your passphrase and your 24-word recovery kit, encrypted files cannot be decrypted. If your Telegram account becomes unavailable or channels are deleted, Telegram-stored backups cannot be retrieved.

Honest Comparison

How Ambry compares to traditional backup.

Every backup model involves real trade-offs between convenience, control, cost, and durability. Choose the model that matches your risk tolerance.

Dimension Ambry (Android) Hosted Cloud Photo Backup Self-Hosted Photo Backup
Infrastructure model No Ambry-operated storage server; Android app talks directly to Telegram via TDLib. Provider-managed cloud servers, web apps, and multi-device sync infrastructure. You run a server (NAS, mini-PC, or VPS) with storage drives, backups, and networking.
Storage backend Private channels in your own dedicated Telegram account (subject to Telegram’s ToS & limits). Commercial cloud object storage with paid tiers and formal service availability commitments. Your own local hard drives, RAID/ZFS pools, or cloud storage buckets you pay for.
Local processing Hashing, EXIF/GPS parsing, thumbnails, albums, memories, and metadata search run on Android. Often processed in the cloud (or on-device in privacy-focused services like Ente). Processed by your home server (enables heavy ML like face clusters and CLIP search).
Encryption options Optional client-side AES-256-GCM encryption, enabled by default; toggleable for new uploads. Varies: standard server-managed encryption on mainstream services, or client-side E2EE on privacy providers. Full disk encryption on your hardware; HTTPS in transit; depends on your server setup.
Server administration None — install the Android app and sign in to your dedicated Telegram account. None — managed entirely by the provider. Ongoing OS updates, Docker/app upgrades, TLS certificates, drive health, and offsite backups.
Recovery & durability You hold the Telegram account + passphrase / 24-word recovery kit. No storage SLA from Telegram. Account recovery flows and professionally replicated storage backed by the provider. Your responsibility to maintain hardware redundancy and off-site copies (e.g., 3-2-1 backup).

Telegram is a messaging platform, not a professional archival storage provider with a durability SLA. For irreplaceable family photos, consider keeping an additional offline or secondary backup copy alongside Ambry.

Technical Transparency

Under the hood, with nothing hidden.

Explore the verified implementation details directly from Ambry’s architecture and engineering logs.

Telegram’s cloud Bot API caps downloads at 20 MB, cannot create channels, cannot list channel history for recovery, and prohibits using bots for external cloud storage sites.

Ambry embeds Telegram’s official TDLib 1.8.65 client library via a native Kotlin/JNI bridge, authenticating with your dedicated Telegram account. This supports single files up to 1.95 GiB, ranged downloads for video streaming, channel creation, message deletion, and history scanning for vault recovery.

Ambry reuses Immich’s Flutter UI and Rust native_core image codecs alongside a local SQLite index (keepsake-index.db) projected into Drift tables.

Your phone runs native SHA-1 hashing for deduplication, MediaStore & EXIF reading, thumbnail (320 px) and preview (1440 px) generation, local metadata search, and a loopback-only HTTP media server (127.0.0.1 guarded by a random per-install path token) to decrypt thumbnails, originals, and HTTP Range video streams on the fly.

Library channel: one document message per backed-up photo or video (sent with disable_content_type_detection when encrypted), accompanied by an encrypted IMTG2 recovery caption containing the wrapped per-file key and essential metadata so the library can be rebuilt even if snapshots are missing.

System channel: a pinned KSVAULT1 vault discovery record (containing only passphrase- and recovery-wrapped vault key blobs), encrypted KSIDX1 SQLite index snapshots (newest 3 kept), and a sealed active-device record enforcing one writer phone per vault.

At setup, Ambry generates a random 256-bit vault key wrapped twice: once with Argon2id (64 MiB, t=3, p=2) from your passphrase, and once with HKDF-SHA256 from your 24-word recovery kit (192 bits). Either secret unlocks the same vault key.

When encryption is enabled (default), each asset receives a fresh random key and is encrypted in 1 MiB AES-256-GCM segments (IMTG v1 format with header + segment index authenticated via AAD), allowing random-access video seeking without downloading the whole file.

File size limit: Single files larger than 1.95 GiB are currently refused (multi-part splitting is not implemented in v1).

Background battery policies: Phones running aggressive background killers (such as Realme/Oppo ColorOS, Xiaomi, or Vivo) may pause closed-app background backups until you allow background activity / “Unrestricted” battery for Ambry. The Backup screen alerts you when this setting is off.

Account or channel loss: If your Telegram account is banned, deleted, or inaccessible, or if you manually delete messages/channels inside Telegram, Ambry cannot retrieve those files from Telegram. Deleting an item permanently inside Ambry’s Trash also deletes the corresponding Telegram message.

Questions & Answers

Straight answers, no fine print.

Everything you should know before trusting Ambry with your Android photo library.

Ambry is an open-source Android photo and video backup app (GNU AGPL v3) adapted from Immich’s mobile application. It lets you browse, organize, and back up your library directly into private channels owned by your own Telegram account, without running a separate storage server.

Ambry embeds Telegram’s official TDLib client library so your phone connects directly to Telegram. After sign-in, Ambry creates two private channels owned by your account: a Library channel for your photos and videos, and a System channel for encrypted index backups. Ambry is an independent project and is not affiliated with Telegram.

We strongly recommend using a dedicated Telegram account rather than your personal messaging account. High-volume media backups can occasionally trigger Telegram rate limits or automated account checks, and a dedicated account keeps your daily chats completely separate.
